# Permissions & Access

dotMARC uses a fine-grained permissions model so internal staff and external clients can be
granted access the same way, by email, with an optional scope rather than an all-or-nothing
login.

## Roles

A **Role** is a named bundle of permissions. Two are built in:

* **Admin**: every permission, locked (its name and permission set can never be changed or
  deleted).
* **Viewer**: read-only (`DomainsView`, `GroupsView`, `TagsView`, `AlertsView`), and limited to
  groups, so it can be restricted to specific Groups.

Admins can also create custom roles covering any subset of the available permissions, domain
management, Group/Tag management, access management, MTA-STS hosting (`MtaStsView`,
`MtaStsManage`), [alerting](./alerts.mdx) (`AlertsView`, `AlertsManage`), DNS provider push
configuration (`DnsPushManage`), viewing the in-app [server logs](./server-logs.mdx)
(`LogsView`), and reading or setting retention for the [audit log](./audit-log.mdx) (`AuditView`,
`AuditManage`) are each independently grantable.

### Roles limited to groups

Switch on **Limit to groups** when you add a custom role, and it can be granted with specific Groups,
like Viewer. Someone with that grant sees and manages only those Groups' domains: staff who look after
particular clients, or a client who manages their own domains. A role's limit can only be switched on
or off while no one holds it.

A limited role can only hold permissions that stay within its Groups: `DomainsView`, `DomainsAdd`,
`DomainsEdit`, `DomainsDelete`, `MtaStsView`, `MtaStsManage`, `GroupsView`, `TagsView`, `AlertsView`,
`AlertsManage`, `AuditView` and `ReportsManage`. Everything else changes something every client
shares, such as other clients' Groups, tags, DNS provider credentials, or who has access. Even if a
limited grant's role somehow holds such a permission, dotMARC drops it at sign-in.

`AlertsManage`, `AuditView` and `ReportsManage` work within the Groups: a limited person can acknowledge
their own Groups' alerts and send their own Groups' client reports, and a limited API key reads only
its own domains' audit entries. The pages those permissions open that cover every client (the audit
log, **Email & reports**, **Alert settings** and **PSA settings**) stay closed to anyone limited to
Groups.

For someone limited to Groups:

* **Manage domains** lists only their Groups' domains and offers only their Groups. A domain they add
  must go into at least one of their Groups, and they can't take a domain out of all of them. A
  domain's other Groups, which they can't see, are left as they are. The PSA company columns are
  hidden.
* **Alerts** shows only their Groups' alerts.
* **DNS push** only works for their Groups' domains.
* **Import domains** and **unparseable messages** aren't available: import works with Group names and
  can create Groups, and unparseable messages belong to no domain. They add domains one at a time.
* A domain dotMARC finds itself, from a report for a domain nobody has added, is in no Group, so only
  people who aren't limited see it until it's put in a Group.

## Granting access

Access is granted from the **Manage Access** page: type an email, pick a role, and if the role
is limited to groups, optionally pick which Groups they can see. This works identically whether the
person is internal staff or an external client contact.

* A grant made before someone's first sign-in stays pending until they actually authenticate, at
  which point it binds to their stable Entra object ID.
* A Viewer scoped to specific Groups can only ever see domains in those Groups enforced at the
  data-query level, not just hidden in the UI, so this holds even via direct URL navigation to a
  domain outside their scope.
* A grant of a limited role with no Groups selected sees every domain, the same as before scoping
  was introduced.
* A Group can't be deleted while it's the only Group someone is limited to, since without it they'd
  see every domain.
* A Group-scoped grant can also be switched to the [client portal](./client-portal.mdx): a simplified,
  branded, read-only view of the client's own domains instead of the dotMARC dashboard.

### Inviting people as Entra guests

Someone from outside your organisation can only sign in once they're a guest in your Entra tenant. With
**Invite to Entra as a guest** ticked when you grant access, dotMARC adds them as a guest for you. Microsoft
doesn't email them: send them dotMARC's address yourself, and they accept the invitation the first time they
sign in. Untick it for people already in your tenant.

This needs the dashboard app registration to hold the Microsoft Graph application permission
`User.Invite.All`, with admin consent. Access is granted whether or not the invite works; if it doesn't,
dotMARC says why (for example, Entra declines to invite someone who's already a member) so you can invite
them by hand. Each invite is recorded in the [audit log](./audit-log.mdx).

API keys are granted a role the same way people are, on the Access page's **API keys** tab. See [API](./api.mdx).

## Bootstrapping the first Admin

See [Getting Started](./getting-started.mdx#configure) and
[Deploy to Azure](./deploy-to-azure.mdx) the `InitialAdmins__Emails` environment variable grants
Admin to the listed emails the very first time the app starts with no existing access grants. This
is the only way to establish the first Admin; set it before your first deploy.
